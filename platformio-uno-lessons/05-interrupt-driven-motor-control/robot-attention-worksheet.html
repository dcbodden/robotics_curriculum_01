Follow the plan and keep checking

Walking, you follow a plan and watch for people or chairs. Robots need fresh information too: has a control changed? Is the path clear? Is the motor too hot? Frequent checks can help avoid collisions and damage.

Today we check the RV control. Obstacles and heat would need other sensors.

Lesson 04: Check, act, and wait

The loop() repeats: read A0 (0–1023), map to PWM (0–255), apply on D3, report the numbers, then wait.

Code pieces, in order:

analogRead(ANALOG_INPUT_PIN)
map(adcReading, ADC_MIN, ADC_MAX, PWM_MIN, PWM_MAX)
analogWrite(MOTOR_PWM_PIN, pwmCommand)
Serial.print(adcReading);
Serial.println(pwmCommand);
delay(CONTROL_UPDATE_DELAY_MS);

The delay is 1000 ms: one second. It is blocking: no new RV reads during the wait. The motor keeps its old PWM power setting.

Move the RV just after a reading: the command can lag by about one second. Just before the next reading, the wait is shorter. An old “go” command could cause a collision.

Lesson 05: A timer reminds the robot to check

Every 100 ms (one tenth of a second), a timer interrupt pauses regular work for a brief checking routine: the ISR. Then regular work continues.

Inside ISR(TIMER1_COMPA_vect):
selected code pieces, in order

analogRead(ANALOG_INPUT_PIN)
map(adcReading, ADC_MIN, ADC_MAX, PWM_MIN, PWM_MAX)
analogWrite(MOTOR_PWM_PIN, pwmCommand)

It reads, maps, and applies the PWM power setting 10 times a second, even with the RV still. PWM's fast on/off pulses have a separate rhythm.

loop() still waits with delay(REPORT_DELAY_MS); (1000 ms). Both print about once a second. ISR checks continue, updating power before the next computer message.
