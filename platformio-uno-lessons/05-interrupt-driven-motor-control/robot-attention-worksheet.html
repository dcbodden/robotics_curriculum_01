Follow the plan and keep checking

Walking, you follow a plan and watch for people or chairs. Robots need fresh information too: has a control changed? Is the path clear? Is the motor too hot? Frequent checks can help avoid collisions and damage.

Today we check the RV control. Obstacles and heat would need other sensors.

Lesson 04: Check, act, and wait

The loop() repeats: read A0 (0–1023), map to PWM (0–255), apply on D3, report the numbers, then wait.

Code pieces, in order:

analogRead(ANALOG_INPUT_PIN)
map(adcReading, ADC_MIN, ADC_MAX, PWM_MIN, PWM_MAX)
analogWrite(MOTOR_PWM_PIN, pwmCommand)
Serial.print(adcReading);
Serial.println(pwmCommand);
delay(CONTROL_UPDATE_DELAY_MS);

The delay is 1000 ms: one second. It is blocking: no new RV reads during the wait. The motor keeps its old PWM power setting.

Move the RV just after a reading: the command can lag by about one second. Just before the next reading, the wait is shorter. An old “go” command could cause a collision.

Lesson 05: A timer reminds the robot to check

Every 100 ms (one tenth of a second), a timer interrupt pauses regular work for a brief checking routine: the ISR. Then regular work continues.

Inside ISR(TIMER1_COMPA_vect):
selected code pieces, in order

analogRead(ANALOG_INPUT_PIN)
map(adcReading, ADC_MIN, ADC_MAX, PWM_MIN, PWM_MAX)
analogWrite(MOTOR_PWM_PIN, pwmCommand)

It reads, maps, and applies the PWM power setting 10 times a second, even with the RV still. PWM's fast on/off pulses have a separate rhythm.

loop() still waits with delay(REPORT_DELAY_MS); (1000 ms). Both print about once a second. ISR checks continue, updating power before the next computer message.

Our investigation question

Will the motor take longer to respond to an RV change when checks are 1000 ms apart than when they are 100 ms apart?

We will listen to and watch the motor to find out.

My hypothesis and reason

A hypothesis is a prediction we can test. Before running either program, write what you think will happen and why, in your own words.

You can begin: “I think the motor will respond … because …”

Experimental steps

  1. Prepare. Use the same B1, RV, and motor circuit. Remove USB and B1 power before wiring. Have your teacher check it. Secure the bare motor with no wheel or propeller; keep its shaft clear.
  2. Run lesson 04. With B1 off, follow its README to build, upload, and open the monitor at 9600 baud.
  3. Set the RV. With B1 off, choose a low position near zero power and a high position. Return to low; turn B1 on after teacher approval.
  4. Test and record on page 3. Just after a computer message, move low to high and hold. Notice how soon the motor responds. Repeat high to low, then low to high, each just after a message.
  5. Run lesson 05. Turn B1 off; leave the wires unchanged. Follow its README to build, upload, and reopen the 9600-baud monitor.
  6. Repeat. With B1 off, return to low. After teacher approval, turn B1 on. Repeat the same three moves just after messages; record on page 3.
  7. Finish safely. Turn B1 off and disconnect USB after closing the monitor. Stop and tell your teacher if anything gets hot or acts unexpectedly.
  8. Conclude. Compare the trial records. Use what you noticed to answer page 3. “Hard to tell” is a useful observation too.
